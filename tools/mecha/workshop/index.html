A generous, bounded economy

Production direction: keep earning one credit per five minutes, but stop accumulation at 50. Spending below the ceiling resumes earning. Preserve existing higher balances and record processed sessions even at the ceiling; discard overflow rather than banking it for later. No daily reset, expiring currency or punishment for time away.

Finishes are reusable unlocks: alloy costs 6 and holo costs 10; individual parts cost 2. Basic readability, reduced motion and contrast remain free. Future arcade entry fees can keep credits useful after the collection is complete, but ordinary launcher functionality never depends on companion progress.

A welcoming first visit

Proposed Hangar introduction: three short, skippable cards—meet your companion; earn and customize; make it yours or hide it. Explain bond, charge and condition without implying that Rivet needs constant attention. Nothing deteriorates while you are away.

The final card should point directly to Animate companion and Hide companion, explain that hiding preserves parts and credits, and let the player opt out immediately. A Help entry reopens the cards later. Explain shipped behaviour only; the workshop’s proposed rewards and arcade do not belong in the introduction yet.

The integration boundary

The browser cabinet above preserves the original experiment; it is not the production payout table. The Android design uses stakes of 1 / 3 / 5, with 20% reward plus returned stake, 30% returned stake, and 50% miss. A random reward therefore costs 25% more on average than its direct price at every tier. Completed tiers stop taking bets; there are no duplicate prizes.

The separate Android Rename change makes the current name non-editable until Rename is pressed; Save or Cancel then exits the editor. These browser demos never read or change your real balance or inventory.
